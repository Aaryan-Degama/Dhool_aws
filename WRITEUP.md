# Dhool: is your rooftop solar actually working?

_Environmental Hacks, Waste and Energy track. Repo: https://github.com/Aaryan-Degama/Dhool_aws · Live app: to be added · Video: to be added_

## The problem

More than 50 lakh Indian households now have rooftop solar under PM Surya Ghar ([PIB, Aug 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2294208)). Their inverter app shows one number a day: units made. It never shows what that roof should have made in that day's weather. So a cloudy day, a dusty panel, a week of smog and a dead string all look the same, and the loss goes unnoticed for months. In Delhi, dust alone costs a roof that waits for rain 7.6% of its energy against one cleaned weekly ([Sādhanā](https://www.ias.ac.in/article/fulltext/sadh/051/0032)). The vendor owes five years of maintenance, but a family cannot claim what it cannot measure.

## What we built

Dhool is a phone web app, in English and Hindi, that turns the daily number into one piece of advice with the reason and the rupees:

- **Wash the panels**, only when the wash pays for itself before the next rain or dust storm.
- **Call your vendor**, with a WhatsApp message carrying the figures, when the drop is a fault and not dust, or the roof made almost nothing on a sunny day.
- **Wait**, when rain will wash them for free, or when it is haze and washing would waste water.
- **Your solar is working.**

Readings come in by a link to the inverter's cloud, a screenshot of the inverter app, or a typed number. Dhool computes what the roof should have made from real weather and solar physics (pvlib, Open-Meteo), learns that roof's own normal, and compares it with the neighbours. After a wash or a vendor visit it measures whether output came back. A family shares one home through a six-letter code. With alerts on, the phone is told when the nightly check turns to "call your vendor" or "wash", or when readings stop.

The verdict is physics and plain rules. No language model decides anything or produces a number.

## Where AWS fits

The whole backend is one template deployed with the **AWS SAM CLI** (an AWS open-source tool), all serverless, in ap-south-1:

- **Lambda:** five functions. Three are container images: the pvlib analysis, a screenshot reader (Qwen3-VL-2B on CPU through llama.cpp) and a vendor-reply reader (Laya, a small multilingual model we fine-tuned, on ONNX Runtime).
- **API Gateway (HTTP API)** with a JWT authorizer, **Cognito** for sign-in.
- **DynamoDB:** one table for homes, family members, readings, verdicts and actions.
- **S3** for screenshots behind signed URLs, **ECR** for the images, **EventBridge** for the nightly analysis (one run per home), **SQS** and **SNS** for failed runs and alarms, **CloudWatch** and **X-Ray** for logs, alarms and tracing.
- The two model functions are pinged every five minutes to stay loaded, so they are not scale-to-zero; everything else is.
- **Amplify Hosting** for the web app.

**Bedrock and Textract were not available on our account**, so we host two small open models on Lambda ourselves and keep all explanations as fixed, hand-checked text. We turned that into a rule: a model may read (a screenshot, a vendor's reply) but never decide, and the homeowner confirms everything a model reads.

## How well it works

- Verdict rules, all in simulation, on a hidden test set in three other cities that we never tuned on. Hardest case first: a home on its own with weather data about 15% wrong a day, **56%**, and almost no faults caught (6 of 433). On its own with exact weather, 83%. Best case, with 29 neighbours and exact weather, **84%** (94–95% on development data). The simulator builds each home from the same physics the rules use, so the best case flatters them; we report the hard case first for that reason.
- Screenshot reader: 93 of 93 values on our demo inverter's screens, but only 6 of 11 on store screenshots of real inverter apps. That is why every number is shown for the homeowner to confirm or correct, and typing is always available. We tried plain OCR in its place: 5 of 11 on the same screenshots with none invented, in a second instead of a minute. One short, so the model stayed.
- Vendor-reply reader: 52 of 60 on an unseen test set.

## What is simulated, and other limits

- **No real inverter brand is connected.** The demo inverter serves 33 simulated homes: real Delhi weather through the physics, with dust, washes and faults planted in code and never generated by a language model.
- The vendor replies that trained the reply reader were written by an AI assistant, not collected. No real vendor reply has been tried.
- On a single real roof with no neighbours to compare with, the rules still say "wash" or "unclear" too often (checked on nine real US rooftops from NREL PVDAQ), and in simulation they miss most faults once the weather data is imperfect. Pooling nearby homes' readings is the fix we would build next, with one real inverter brand's connector.
- With no rain or dust storm in the forecast, a wash is assumed to keep paying for 30 days. That is an assumption, not a measurement.

## AI tools used

Claude Code (Anthropic) was used throughout for code, tests, the Hindi text and documentation. No AI model runs in the app except the two self-hosted readers named above.
